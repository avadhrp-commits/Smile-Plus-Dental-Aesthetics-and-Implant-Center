What Is Root Canal Treatment?

Saving your tooth, not removing it.

Root canal treatment (RCT) removes infected or inflamed pulp tissue from inside a tooth — usually caused by deep decay, a crack, or repeated dental procedures on the same tooth. The canal is then cleaned, shaped, disinfected and sealed, saving the natural tooth instead of extracting it.

The biggest misconception is that RCT itself hurts. In reality, local anaesthesia numbs the area completely before treatment begins — the discomfort people associate with root canals almost always comes from the untreated infection beforehand, not the procedure that fixes it.

Most straightforward cases at Smile+ are completed in a single, calm sitting, with a protective crown recommended afterward to restore full strength.

Signs You May Need One

When it's time for a check-up, not a wait.

  • Lingering pain when you bite down or after hot and cold food.
  • Swelling or tenderness in the gum near a specific tooth.
  • A tooth that has visibly darkened compared to the others.
  • A cracked tooth or a deep cavity that's reached the nerve.

Common Questions

Root canal treatment, myth-free.

Is root canal treatment really as painful as people say?

This is one of the most outdated dental myths still searched today. Modern root canal treatment is done under local anaesthesia with precise, guided instruments — most patients describe it as no more uncomfortable than getting a filling. The pain people remember is usually from the infection itself, before treatment.

How many sittings does root canal treatment take?

Many straightforward cases at Smile+ are completed in a single sitting. More complex infections or curved canals may need two visits, which Dr. Yashasvi Patel will confirm after examining your X-ray.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

In most cases, yes — a crown protects the treated tooth, which becomes more brittle after the procedure, and restores full biting strength. Your dentist will advise if your specific tooth needs one.

What happens if I ignore an infected tooth instead of treating it?

An untreated infection can spread to the bone around the tooth, cause an abscess, and eventually mean the tooth can't be saved at all, requiring extraction. Treating it early with a root canal is almost always the more comfortable and affordable path.
